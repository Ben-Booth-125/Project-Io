WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW (Joe, 2026-09-01)
- The map must look SEMI-REALISTIC / representational — not schematic, not
  abstract. Real-ish terrain, real-ish models.
- NO hex grid on screen unless a tile is selected. A selected tile shows a
  single hex outline; nothing else is gridded.
- The camera ROTATES / TILTS as you zoom in: from near-orbital top-down out
  wide, down to a low oblique angle up close, where you see models and terrain
  "as if you were standing on the planet".

  (EARNED / selection), saturated cyan #3FC9E8 (secondary). Shell chrome is the
  settled U2 / menu-shell "final" direction — but this round is CHROME-STRIPPED,
  terrain art only.

ROUND 1 — 5-WAY ART-DIRECTION COMPARISON SHEET  (output: it2)
=============================================================================
One wide 16:9 image, 5 equal vertical panels, thin near-black (#0F0F14)
gutters, a small stencil label (A B C D E) top-left of each panel.

SHARED ACROSS ALL 5 PANELS (only the art direction changes):
- Same patch of alien-but-earthlike planetary terrain: rolling hills, a river
  valley, patchy conifer-like forest, exposed rock, a small industrial
  settlement of blocky low-rise buildings with a couple of smoking stacks.
- Same camera: low oblique, ~35-40 degrees above horizontal — the tilted
  near-surface angle the game reaches after zooming in. NOT top-down.
- Same lighting direction and time of day in every panel.
- Exactly ONE terrain tile selected, marked by a single glowing amber
  (#E8A33D) hexagon outline on the ground. No other hex grid anywhere.
- No UI chrome, no icons, no text overlays. Dark near-black surroundings.
  Flat, no vignette.

THE 5 DIRECTIONS:
A  Orbital orthophoto realism
   Photoreal satellite-style terrain, natural colour, crisp, minimal
   stylisation. The "real planet from a sat feed" look.

B  Diorama / scale model
   Terrain reads as a sculpted physical tabletop model — tactile handmade
   materials, very slight miniature depth-of-field. Warm, tactile.

C  Painterly relief
   Hand-painted topographic look, exaggerated relief and hillshade, softened
   detail. Old World / Humankind family.

D  Arid near-future
   Desaturated dusty palette, atmospheric haze, low sun with long shadows,
   sparse vegetation. Reads as a colony world, not lush Earth.

E  Realism + diegetic survey overlay
   Photoreal ground like A, plus faint pale contour lines, small survey tick
   marks and coordinate ticks baked into the ground at low opacity — a
   corporate survey map.

HOW IT WAS RENDERED
- Joe rendered this one on GPT (free unlimited image credits at the time),
  not Higgs. Prompt above pasted in full. Output: it2.

ROUND 1 VERDICTS (2026-09-01, Joe + Claude read)
- A  orthophoto realism — functional baseline. Legible, real-place read,
     buildings/terrain clear. A bit generic. The safe default.
- B  diorama — REJECTED as rendered. Miniature depth-of-field blur is
     disqualifying for a map you read constantly (mushy foreground). Sculpted
     tactile materials were the good part; not worth pursuing unless that look
     is specifically wanted, and then with NO DOF.
- C  painterly relief — STRONGEST looking. Exaggerated hillshade makes
     topography readable at a glance, brushwork has character, still reads
     semi-realistic, buildings stay legible. Looks like a game, not a photo.
- D  arid near-future — mood is right and most on-theme, BUT it swapped the
     biome for desert. Near-future is a GRADE (desaturated palette + haze +
     low sun + long shadows) to apply over ANY biome, not a terrain type.
- E  realism + survey overlay — overlay is tasteful (faint ticks, coord
     labels, contours, doesn't fight the terrain) but it is just A + a layer.
     Treat as an optional toggle on the winning base, not a base style.
- Selected-tile amber (#E8A33D) hex glow sits convincingly on the terrain at
  the oblique angle in all 5 panels. That treatment is confirmed.

--- PANEL SET 1: 2x2 GRADE MATRIX  (output: it3) ---
One image, 4 equal panels, labelled top-left:
  A-N  orthophoto realism, NEUTRAL grade (natural colour, high sun).
  A-F  orthophoto realism, NEAR-FUTURE grade: desaturated, cool cast,
       distance haze, low sun with long shadows. Same biome — only the grade
       changes from A-N.
  C-N  painterly relief, NEUTRAL grade.
  C-F  painterly relief, NEAR-FUTURE grade (same grade recipe as A-F).
Judge: which base (A or C), and whether -F earns its place or -N is enough.

ROUND 2 VERDICTS (it3, 2026-09-01, Joe — PROVISIONAL, pending Ben)
- Panels barely diverged: GPT clamped to the it2 composition and would not
  push "painterly" far while the frame was held fixed. A-N and C-N came out
  nearly the same. Loosen the fixed-composition constraint from here on.
- FRONT-RUNNER: C-F (painterly relief + near-future grade). The lack of
  saturation reads colder / more hostile / serious — the tone the game wants.
- C-N "very nice" but too warm and inviting for the game.
- A-F does a good job but is a tad too realistic / photoreal.
- CONFIRMED: near-future is a separable GRADE (desaturate + haze + low sun),
  not a biome. It can be a runtime pass over any base.
- Base family = C (painterly relief); grade = F (near-future). Provisional.
