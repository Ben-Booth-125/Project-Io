**We build logistics and set demands for goods — then we build companies on top of that.**

`COLONISATION.md` is the peopling of an empty world and `CIVILISATION.md` is the empires that
contested it. This document owns the span after the dark age: **1560 → 1960**, in which the
nations an empire left behind reach across water, and the world arrives at the epoch already
wanting things it cannot make. It is the **demand side** of the campaign, generated as history.

**The premise, in Ben's words (2026-09-09):** *"If we have certain deposits in certain locations, a
colonial round would be establishing diplomatic or military presence to claim territory. The
colonial round tells you what types of endemic resources cultures are interested in, how wealthy
certain nations are, and gives us specific targets for corporations, based on the strength of the
prior trade network."* And the ordering that follows: **the corporations come after.** A round
that set demand and placed firms in one breath would be two subjects in one story, exactly the cut
that fused rounds 3 and 4 until they were watched apart (`../ui/STARTUP.md` § Round 4 — Empires).

## The span, and the engine it runs on

The colonial era is **pass 2** of the three passes `GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § Three passes of
simulated history names: **1560 → 1960**, on the same polity engine as pass 1, entered from the
coast that follows 1200 CE. The calendar is stated, not derived, and the era-band flip it implies
is owned with it (`GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § Pass 2 is the economy pass).

**Same engine, a different question.** Pass 1 asks who holds what ground. This span asks what that
ground *is for* — which polities light a furnace and when, who reaches which shore, who comes to
want what — and it reads the ground as **capital** for the first time. `CIVILISATION.md` § What this
phase hands the industrial era draws that line deliberately: the empire phase abstracts resources
into what conquest consumes; this one is where a deposit becomes a reason to cross a sea.

**The inputs are already on the table.** Tiles, provinces, regions and their endowment, nations as
the fold of the polity map, the biosphere's endemic goods with their latitude band and sector, the
ancient road network and the trade junctions the empires left (`../economy/LOGISTICS.md` § 4a). This
span invents none of them. It reads them and returns *relationships*: who wants what, who is rich,
and which water is cheap to cross.

**It never runs the economy tick.** A quarterly tick costs 530–710 ms; four hundred years of them
is fourteen to nineteen minutes. The colonial era therefore produces no prices, clears no market and
places no firm. Everything it hands forward is a scalar or a stamped tile the static search and
the live economy read as an input — which is what keeps pass 3 a search with no clock
(`GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § Phase 6 is a STATIC SEARCH).

## Two ways to claim ground across water

A colonial claim is a polity verb on a target across a **sea leg**, staged from harbour works, and
there are **two of them**. Both are the same actor at the same grain, choosing among its sim verbs,
and both sit inside the 2026-08-18 nation-and-polity grant on the terms that grant sets — pure,
seeded, deterministic, scored-utility, never a planner (`../ai/AI_OPPONENT.md` § 11). The second
verb was **raised** by Ben on 2026-09-09, not read into the first.

**Purchase — the diplomatic claim.** *"Something like purchasing a province for the purpose of
trade, and respecting local customs."* A polity pays for a province rather than taking it, out of
the stores its seat already accumulates (`CIVILISATION.md` § Materials are spent when something
happens — the same currency, no second one). What it buys is *access*: the province's endowment
becomes reachable to the buyer's network, and the buyer comes to want what it grows. What it does
**not** buy is the people. **A purchased province keeps its culture shares**; nothing is digested,
because nothing was conquered. That is what "respecting local customs" means as data.

**Conquest — the military claim.** *"Military claims, and war for items such as gold."* The
campaign verb `MILITARY_HISTORY.md` already resolves, played across a sea leg. The province changes
hands, its people are digested over centuries exactly as `GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § What pass 1
hands back describes, and the grudge ledger records who did it. A conquered province's endowment is
the conqueror's outright, which is why a polity would fight for it: gold is worth a war in a way a
cash crop's *access* is not.

**The scorer chooses between them on the ground's terms**, never on a posture flag. A rich
metal endowment behind a weak defence scores the campaign; a cash-crop province under a polity with
a full store scores the purchase; a seller whose stores are empty is cheaper to buy from. The two
verbs are one contest with two costs, which is what makes the map readable afterwards — a player
can point at a colony and say *bought* or *taken*, and the culture shares and the grudge ledger
will agree.

**Both culminate as every major does.** The non-hegemony invariant is not relaxed for the sea; a
metropole that overreaches fragments (`../lore/COLLAPSE.md`), and its colonies arrive at 1960 as
nations with a grudge, a taste and a sea lane.

## Who discovered what — taste is a fact of history

**The endemic goods already have a demand channel**, and it is the consumer this span was missing.
`../economy/RESOURCES.md` § Mercantile — endemic trade goods gives the cash crops their value
track, and the endemic trade channel injects a wealth-scaled, character-flavoured want for them
(`../economy/MARKETS.md` § Demand channels). What has been derived from national character alone
becomes, here, a **consequence of who reached what**.

**A polity discovers a good when its network first reaches a region carrying that good's source** —
by purchase, by conquest, or by supply reach from ground it already holds. Discovery is a dated
event with a place, like a battle, and it is recorded as one. From it the *metropole's* people
acquire the taste: the nation that inherits the discovering polity carries a weight for that good
into the endemic channel, and its households want it in proportion to their wealth. **The colony
grows it; the metropole wants it.** That asymmetry is the whole reason a colonial trade exists,
and it is why the taste lands on the discoverer and not on the ground.

**A nation that discovered nothing wants nothing exotic**, and that falls out rather than being
special-cased. A landlocked polity on a continent with no cash crop arrives at 1960 with a
household basket that names none — which is a fact about its history the player can read, not a
penalty.

**What this is not.** It is not a demand basket injected by generation into market arrays. The
channel is the mechanism and it already exists; this span moves its *weights*, and the rule in
`../economy/MARKETS.md` § Demand channels — a consumer is a mechanism, not a noun — holds unchanged.

## How wealthy a nation is — inequality is furthered, never flattened

**Every nation opens the campaign with a treasury, and the balance is a fact of this span.** A
polity accumulates a **wealth scalar** across 1560 → 1960, moved deterministically by what it did
and what it held: how early it lit a furnace against its neighbours (the axis the tariff posture
already reads, `../politics/NATIONS.md` § 4), the endemic sources its network reached, the
provinces it bought and the spoils of the ones it took, and the sea lanes it kept open. At
handoff the scalar seeds the nation's opening balance and is the wealth term the endemic channel
scales its want by.

**The spread is the deliverable.** `GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § Asymmetry is the deliverable already
forbids clamping any start up to viability or down to fairness, and this span is where the
inequality it asks for is *furthered*: a metropole that reached three shores is richer than the
city state it bought from, and the difference is legible on the map. No world is steered to a Gini;
the sweep reports the distribution and asserts only that it is not degenerate.

**What it is not.** Wealth here is not a price, a market or a firm. It is a scalar per polity
derived at the end of the span, in the same shape as `polity::protection_q` — computed once,
after the loop, read by nothing inside it.

## The colonial tie is a sea lane, and the map reads it

**A tie is a force on the map, never a preference inside an actor.** The earlier design seeded the
order book's preferred-seller relationships from ties; that half is superseded (Ben, 2026-09-09),
because the buy side that would have read them is dormant in play and a relationship nothing reads
is not a force. What the design needed was the same thing the ancient roads gave the land.

**Sea lanes are stamped FROM the colonial record, exactly as ancient roads are stamped from the
empires'.** The span records every sea leg it walked — a purchase party's crossing, a campaign's
sea supply, the standing traffic between a metropole and what it holds — and a pass immediately
after it stamps those legs onto the water as a **sea lane** tier that discounts the sea-leg
traversal cost, the water analogue of `../economy/LOGISTICS.md` § 4a. Traffic earns the tier; a
crossing made once is no lane. Purely additive; land tiles are untouched.

**What reads it is everything that reads traversal cost**, because traversal cost is one weight
function (`../economy/LOGISTICS.md` § 1). A convoy between a colony's market and its metropole's
is cheaper than one to a stranger's, so a colony's chains close through its metropole *first*
without anyone naming a preferred seller — the landed price says so. The same discount widens
**placement reach** across the lane, so a firm on one shore can legally hold a site on the other.
That is a wider effect than a market preference and it is the intended one: a tie that only moved
prices would be invisible to the search, and a search that cannot see roads cannot see lanes.

**A lane is what a colony leaves behind when the metropole falls.** Ties outlive the polity that
made them, as roads outlive the empire that paved them; the map at 1960 carries lanes between
nations that have not been one polity for a century, which is the trade network the next section
reads.

## What this span hands the corporate search

**Targets, not firms.** The search that selects a corporate landscape (`GENERATION_STRATEGY.md`
§ The eight phases, phase 6) starts from one seed candidate and refines it greedily. Where that
seed comes from was the half nothing owned, and this span owns it: **the strength of the prior
trade network** — corridor traffic, sea-lane traffic and junction degree — is what places the seed
candidate's firms. A junction three corridors and a lane meet at is a target; a rich province
nothing reaches is not, yet. The perturbation axes are unchanged; only the starting point stops
being a placement seed and becomes a fact of history.

**The data contract**, and it is a struct, not a promise — the discipline pass 1 already keeps
(`GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § What crosses each handoff, and nothing else):

| What | Shape | Who reads it |
|---|---|---|
| The polity map at 1960 | which polity held each region | the nation fold (`GENERATION_STRATEGY.md` § What crosses each handoff, and nothing else) |
| Claims, by mode | per province: bought or taken, from whom, when | culture shares, the grudge ledger, round 5 |
| Discoveries | per polity: which endemic goods, where, when | the endemic demand channel's per-nation weights |
| Wealth | one scalar per polity | the opening treasury; the endemic channel's wealth term |
| Protection, with the colony term | `polity::protection_q` | the `import_tariff` law at setup (`../politics/NATIONS.md` § 4) |
| Sea lanes | a traffic count per sea leg, stamped to a tier | traversal cost; every consumer of it |
| Network strength | corridor and lane traffic, junction degree per region | the search's seed candidate |

Everything in the table is a scalar, a count or a stamped tile. Nothing is a price.

## Round 5 is a still, and the corporations are round 6

**The wizard shows this span as its own round, and it is a still** (Ben, 2026-09-09): the pass
computes, then four objects draw in order on the epoch globe — **metros grown** from the centres
the empires sacked and raised, **claims by mode** (bought and taken, coloured apart), **sea lanes**,
and on the left where round 4 kept its leaderboard, **who wants what and who is rich**. Not a
time-lapse: four hundred years on a map whose borders barely move would be a frozen globe — worse
than a bar, not better (`../ui/STARTUP.md` § The wait is the round, not a loading bar).

**The corporations are the round after.** Firm markers with their charters and the market carve
with its price field — the two objects the earlier round 5 carried — move to **round 6**, drawn
from the search's winner. Demand first, then the companies that answer it; the player watches the
cause before the consequence. The screens are `../ui/STARTUP.md`'s (§ Round 5 — The colonial
era; § Round 6 — Corporations).

## Cost — measured, and where the wait actually is

Measured 2026-09-09 at `/O2` (`history_span_cost`, seed 0): the polity engine runs 400 years in
**0.9 s** at 1,742 regions and 4,000 years in **66 s** at 3,734, with reach 34–55% of the run. The
colonial span is 400 years at the *end-of-span* region count, so it is expected in the low seconds
and is **measured on landing, never assumed**: sea legs widen the neighbour graph, and the
densification NR-809 measured is the one term that could make it otherwise.

**The wait the player feels is not this span.** `world_determinism` puts the post-era passes at
10–25 s and the era at under 3 s in its short runs; the warm start is 72 s and is retired by the
search. A still that draws in seconds after a pass that ran in seconds is a round; the unwatched
bar behind Begin is where the time goes, and it is `../ui/STARTUP.md`'s to close.

## Open questions

- **What a purchase costs**, in the seat's stores, and whether the seller's price rises with what
  its stores already hold. A measurement over the sweep, not a number chosen to make one seed
  buy.
- **Whether supply reach alone discovers**, or only a claim does. Reach alone makes a coastal
  polity discover everything on its own continent, which may be right and may make every
  neighbour want the same three goods.
- **What the wealth scalar is made of**, term by term, and whether spoils from conquest should
  outweigh access from purchase — the sweep's spread is the test, and a flat spread is the only
  trigger for reweighting.
- **Whether a lane decays** when the traffic that earned it stops. Roads do not (`../economy/LOGISTICS.md`
  § 4a); the sea is not a roadbed, and a lane may be the one network element that should.
