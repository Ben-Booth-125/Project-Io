The dossier behind the campaign's framing. Sources are the standard ones for the period;
everything legendary or contested is flagged where it appears, and collected again at the end.
"Year zero" means the window of Augustus's mature principate and the last Western Han decade,
roughly 10 BC – AD 10 (there is no calendar year 0; 1 BC passes straight to AD 1).

## Rome — expansion as divine mandate

**The principate is a religious settlement.** Octavian took the name *Augustus* (27 BC) — a
word Romans connected to *augur* and *augere*, "to increase": the title is itself a theological
claim. From 12 BC he is also *pontifex maximus*; head of state and head of state religion are
one office, permanently.

**War planning happens inside a temple.** From 2 BC the Senate debates declarations of war in
the temple of Mars Ultor — Mars the Avenger — in the Forum of Augustus, where the recovered
standards live. The legionary eagle is a sacred object; losing one is sacrilege demanding
vengeance, which is how Rome narrates both the Parthian standards (returned 20 BC, celebrated
as a cosmic event) and the three eagles lost with Varus (AD 9).

**The theology of no limit.** Virgil's Jupiter promises *imperium sine fine* — empire without
end in space or time (*Aeneid* 1.278–9); the mission statement is *parcere subiectis et
debellare superbos* — spare the submissive, war down the proud (6.851–3). The Secular Games of
17 BC ritually restarted cosmic time under Augustus; his own *Res Gestae* frames every conquest
as pacification of the world-disc.

**State religion is military mechanics.** No legitimate campaign without auspices — and after
19 BC every campaign is effectively fought under Augustus's auspices (the last triumph by
anyone outside the family is Balbus's, 19 BC): a god-driven command hierarchy, ready-made.
Augustus burned thousands of freelance prophecies in circulation, culled the Sibylline Books —
the state's oracle archive — themselves, and moved them to his own Palatine temple: custody of
the future, seized.

**Evocatio — the crown jewel for our framing.** Roman doctrine held that a siege is won by
formally inviting the enemy city's god to defect — a temple and cult in Rome in exchange for
the walls. Canonical cases (Veii 396 BC, Carthage 146 BC) are legendary in their details; the
one epigraphic witness is a 75 BC inscription from Isaura Vetus, and how often the rite was
actually used is contested. But the *doctrine* is the point: Rome's theology of war includes
converting your gods into their soldiers. Its dark twin, *devotio*, dedicates an enemy army —
or a Roman commander's own life — to the gods below.

**The frontiers, c. 10 BC – AD 9.** Germania: Drusus to the Elbe (12–9 BC), Tiberius's
campaigns, the *immensum bellum* (AD 1–5), the planned pincer on Maroboduus aborted by the
Pannonian revolt (AD 6–9), then Teutoburg (AD 9). The Danube: Pannonia conquered, Moesia
organized. The East: armed diplomacy with Parthia dressed as conquest, and the proxy contest
over Armenia. At the exact hinge of eras, Rome fully expected Germania to become a province —
expansion was not a policy among policies; it was the state's proof of divine favour.

## Han — preservation as cosmology

**The court at year zero is transferring the throne by omen.** Emperor Ai dies childless
(1 BC); Wang Mang rules as regent for the child Emperor Ping, then as "Acting Emperor" for an
infant, and in AD 9 proclaims the Xin dynasty on a cascade of manufactured portents — a white
stone with red writing, bronze caskets carrying Heaven's mandate-tokens (*fuming*). (Ping's
death by poison is contemporary rumour, unproven; the omens are genuine period documents *and*
propaganda by design.) Power at year zero is justified not by conquest but by documented signs
from Heaven.

**The Mandate is a calibration, not a licence.** *Tianming* — Heaven appoints the virtuous and
withdraws favour through floods, droughts, eclipses; the correlative cosmology traditionally
credited to Dong Zhongshu (the attribution is looser than the textbooks say) makes misrule
literally a cosmic disturbance. The Son of Heaven's job is correct calendar, correct
rites, correct sacrifices. Omens are strategic intelligence; a portent is Heaven moving troops.

**Why the posture is preservation.** Emperor Wu's expansions nearly bankrupted the state, and
the Salt and Iron debates (81 BC) fixed the consensus: aggressive war is fiscally and morally
corrosive. Defence is infrastructure — wall lines, the Hexi corridor garrisons, *tuntian*
soldier-farmer colonies. The steppe is handled by ritual, not war: after the Xiongnu civil war
the chanyu attends court as tributary (51 BC) and receives a bride as a gift from superior to
inferior (33 BC) — the old appeasement *heqin* reversed into ritual subordination. The one
famous aggressive stroke, Chen Tang's unauthorized 36 BC expedition, is remembered precisely
because it was exceptional — and the court debated punishing him for it.

**The civilising mission — theirs.** The ideal is *laihua* — the distant "come to be
transformed" by the emperor's virtue, presenting tribute, receiving richer gifts. The map is
imagined as five concentric submission zones radiating from the throne: nothing needs
conquering, because everything already, in principle, belongs to *tianxia* — All under Heaven.

**And ours.** Rome's is the same claim inverted: *parcere subiectis et debellare superbos*, and
a world-disc pacified rather than a world arriving. Both are civilising missions; they disagree
only about who travels. That is the campaign's engine — we go out to transform, they wait to be
arrived at, and each reads the other's method as impiety.

## The ideological war

The two empires never fought and barely knew of each other — rumours along silk routes brokered
by Parthia; the first recorded Chinese attempt to reach Rome is AD 97. The campaign is a
counterfactual collision, and says so. As *systems* they are perfect mirror-antagonists:

| | Rome | Han |
|---|---|---|
| Universal claim | *Imperium sine fine* — Jupiter grants boundless rule | *Tianxia* — All under Heaven is already the emperor's |
| Direction of force | Centrifugal: legions go out to make the claim true | Centripetal: virtue radiates; the periphery comes in |
| Victory condition | *Debellare superbos* — the proud warred down | *Laihua* — the distant transformed, arriving with tribute |
| God-war mechanic | *Evocatio*: capture the enemy's gods; auspices license each campaign | *Tianming*: Heaven redeploys favour; omens are intelligence |
| Failure state | Lost eagles — sacrilege demanding vengeance | Portents — Heaven withdrawing the Mandate, no battle needed |
| Each narrates the other | Another proud kingdom not yet under the auspices; its gods candidates for evocatio | Distant outer-zone barbarians — possibly virtuous ones |

The asymmetry the campaign plays: Rome's gods send soldiers outward and are proven right only
by expansion; Heaven withholds soldiers except to restore harmony and is proven right only by
stability. **Each side's win condition is the other's evidence of impiety.**

## The other boards (kept in reserve)

- **Zoroastrian Parthia** — loosely Zoroastrian (the Arsacids were religiously eclectic in
  practice, Greek gods on their coinage) — Rome's only peer competitor and gatekeeper of the
  silk road; kingship rests on *khvarenah*, the divine radiance that abandons usurpers — a
  functional cousin of the Mandate. (The court scandal of Musa and Phraates V rests on
  Josephus alone.)
- **Second-Temple Judaism** — Judaea becomes a province AD 6; the census revolt's doctrine holds
  that Caesar's tax denies God's kingship; Qumran's War Scroll scripts angelic armies in
  formation — the era's most explicit gods-sending-soldiers text.
- **Druidic Gaul and Britain** — a trans-tribal priesthood Rome cannot co-opt: at year zero
  Augustus merely bars citizens from it; full suppression follows under Tiberius and Claudius
  (Anglesey, AD 60). The human-sacrifice allegations come entirely from hostile Roman sources,
  and the campaign repeats them only *as* Roman propaganda, which is period-accurate.
- **Germania** — cult in groves, divination by lots and horses, and the vowed destruction of a
  defeated enemy deposited in bogs (archaeologically solid); god-names for this date are
  retrojections from Tacitus (AD 98) and later. Arminius is a Roman-trained equestrian — the
  frontier's ideologies were porous in both directions.
- **Footnotes:** the Xiongnu chanyu's own title claims Heaven's sonship (a rival steppe Mandate;
  Tengri-cult reading partly reconstructed); Buddhism has not yet reached China (the entry legend —
  Emperor Ming's dream — is later tradition; the first documentary trace is AD 65, an edict
  mentioning Buddhists at the Chu court); the Isis cult is the Roman world's spreading personal-salvation
  religion.

## Contested and legendary, collected

- Veii evocatio details (Livy): legendary embroidery on a possibly-real rite; frequency of
  evocatio at all is debated (Isaura Vetus 75 BC is the only inscription; Macrobius is 5th-c.).
- Emperor Ping's poisoning: rumour, unproven. Wang Mang's omens: authentic documents,
  manufactured content.
- Musa marrying her son: Josephus only.
- Druidic human sacrifice: hostile sources; archaeology suggestive, not conclusive.
- Germanic religion c. 0 AD: mostly retrojected from Tacitus and archaeology.
- Han–Rome mutual knowledge: effectively nil at year zero; the admiring Chinese descriptions of
  "Da Qin" are later (1st–5th c. AD).
